## Final prompt

```text
Create a publication-quality editorial scientific illustration that makes the
Dynamics Atlas Scientific Evidence Harness understandable at first glance: an
Agent may propose molecular evidence, but deterministic comparability gates
decide whether the evidence can support the claim.

Use one continuous scene on a bright warm-ivory scientific workbench. Begin
with two visibly different molecular evidence sources: a protein-ligand

or structural specimen card with provenance. Let a small neutral Agent

Pass the evidence through six visibly distinct precision gates for source
identity, citation readiness, residue-axis mapping, mapping coverage,
measurement and time semantics, and maturity plus claim scope. Make the
residue-mapping correspondence threads the visual focal point. Divert
incompatible mappings or time semantics into review/reject trays. End with a
sealed, hash-like evidence bundle and a restrained claim-scope gauge inspected
by a human scientist.

Blend accurate protein ribbons, trajectory traces, evidence cards, etched
glass, brushed metal, and brass calibration rings. Use warm ivory, graphite,
muted teal and green for compatible evidence, amber for provenance, and
terracotta for incompatibility. Do not use purple, blue-black, neon, a
humanoid robot, AI brain, chatbot, cyberpunk UI, decorative DNA, glossy SaaS
cards, a node-link graph, readable prose, logos, or a watermark.

Do not imply that structural similarity proves mechanism, thermodynamics, or
kinetics. The Agent proposes; deterministic validation owns comparability and
claim scope.
```
